# Stage 3 · Solution Aware

*The prospect's silent question: "Which kind of solution is right, and why?"*

**Definition:** The person knows that solutions exist and may know several categories or methods, but has not chosen an approach and may not know your product as the best match.

**Primary-source nuance (Schwartz):** Crystallise the desired result and its solution, prove that the solution can be achieved, then show that the mechanism of achievement is contained in the product.

## What is happening in the prospect's mind

The person is exploring and evaluating — building a shortlist and decision criteria. The question is no longer whether improvement is possible, but which mechanism, method or provider offers the preferred outcome with acceptable trade-offs.

- The person compares approaches, not only brands.
- Confusion rises because categories use overlapping and incompatible claims.
- Clear criteria, demonstration, authority and honest trade-offs reduce evaluation effort.
- Differentiation must become a mechanism or decision criterion, not an adjective such as "better".

## The ad's single primary job

**Belief shift:** From "Several solutions may work" → To "This solution type and these criteria make the most sense, and this product fits them."

Teach the person how to choose. Establish the relevant criteria, explain the mechanism and show why the product fulfils those criteria without hiding material trade-offs.

## Ad architecture

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hook | Category question, comparison, criterion, myth or "before you choose" |
| Criteria | Define three to five requirements based on the prospect's desired result and risk |
| Mechanism | Show how the solution works and why that mechanism affects the outcome |
| Proof | Demonstration, expert evidence, reviews, cases or specific outcomes |
| Close | Compare, see a demo, get a recommendation or request an estimate |

Default sequence: Decision question → criteria → mechanism → comparison → proof. Typical CTA: compare, see demo, get recommendation.

## Hook and angle families (English patterns)

- "Electric crib or manual bedside crib? Choose based on the nights you actually have."
- "Fine line, realism or black and grey? Start with how the motif should age, not only the style name."
- "Lead form or booking page? It depends on how much of the decision the prospect must make before contact."
- "Before choosing curtains, compare light, privacy and maintenance — not only colour."

## Danish phrasing patterns (write like this, do not translate)

## Creative, proof, CTA and destination

| Component | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Creative | Comparison, demonstration, checklist, expert breakdown, mechanism video, buyer's guide or process case |
| Proof | Product in use, measurable detail, verified reviews, qualified authority and realistic cases |
| CTA | Compare, see the demo, find your solution, get a recommendation, calculate or request an estimate |
| Destination | Category page, comparison page, product selector, detailed service page or case hub |
| Message match | The destination must continue the same criteria instead of switching to a generic brand homepage |

## Schwartz processes that carry this stage

- **Mechanization:** name a familiar mechanism, describe an unfamiliar one, or feature the mechanism when sophistication demands it — the credible "how".
- **Concentration:** identify a genuine weakness in the standard alternative and prove the product removes it; fair side-by-side, no straw men.
- **Verification:** place tests, data, demonstration, testimonials or authority exactly where the claim creates demand for proof.

## Common mistakes

- Continuing to argue that the problem exists.
- Listing features without linking them to a decision criterion.
- Using unfair comparisons or a deliberately weak version of the alternative.
- Hiding the mechanism behind words such as premium, innovative or tailored.
