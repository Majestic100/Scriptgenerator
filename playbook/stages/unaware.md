# Stage 1 · Completely Unaware

*The prospect's silent question: "Why should this matter to me?"*

**Definition:** The person does not recognise the issue as a problem, is not actively considering a solution and may not know the category exists. A latent desire, identity or familiar situation may still provide a credible point of entry.

**Primary-source nuance (Schwartz):** Do not lead with price, product name, product function or a direct statement of the desire. Enter through an accepted state of mind: an unspoken dream, fear, identification, resentment, desired triumph or the visible consequence of the problem. The headline sells the next line; the body gradually bridges to desire, problem, solution and product.

## What is happening in the prospect's mind

Unaware does not mean uninterested in life. The person has not organised their experience using the problem language the business uses. Product names, features and discounts lack mental context.

- Attention goes to familiar situations, emotions, identities, surprising observations and stories.
- The primary resistance is often indifference rather than a defined objection.
- A latent desire may be stronger than a problem: calm, pride, status, convenience, control or creative self-expression.
- An Unaware hook must still qualify logical prospects. Irrelevant spectacle attracts attention while reducing message-market fit.

## The ad's single primary job

**Belief shift:** From "This has nothing to do with me" → To "I recognise that pattern, and it is worth understanding."

Create relevance before explanation. Begin in the world the person already inhabits, then reveal an overlooked cause, cost or possibility. The product may appear as payoff or evidence, but not as assumed knowledge.

## Ad architecture

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hook | Situation, identity, surprising observation, visual moment or open story |
| Recognition | Make the pattern concrete through one scene, symptom or contrast |
| Bridge | Name the problem, desire or opportunity only after recognition exists |
| Product role | Introduce the product briefly as a new path; demonstrate more than you explain |
| Close | Low-friction next step: see how, get ideas, learn more or take a quiz |

Default sequence: Familiar situation → recognition → new meaning → light product bridge. Typical CTA: see how, learn more, explore.

## Hook and angle families (English patterns)

- "It is rarely the night itself that feels long. It is every small time you have to get up again."
- "The hardest part of getting a tattoo is not the pain. It is choosing something you will still like in ten years."
- "Three signs your curtains are working against the room — without you noticing."
- "I thought that was simply how it had to be. Then I changed one thing."

## Danish phrasing patterns (write like this, do not translate)

- "Det er sjældent selve natten, der føles lang. Det er alle de små gange, du skal op igen."
- "Den sværeste del ved en tattoo er ikke smerten. Det er at vælge noget, du stadig kan lide om ti år."
- "Tre tegn på, at jeres gardiner arbejder imod rummet - uden at I lægger mærke til det."
- "Jeg troede, det bare var sådan, det var. Så ændrede jeg én ting."

## Creative, proof, CTA and destination

| Component | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Creative | Story-led UGC, POV, day in the life, founder observation, visual demonstration or explained before/after |
| Proof | Recognisable reality and concrete observation; heavy testimonial proof can follow later |
| CTA | See how, get ideas, read the guide, try the quiz. A purchase CTA may be tested, but cannot be the whole argument |
| Destination | Short guide, advertorial, quiz, inspiration page or product page that continues the same story |
| Tempo | Communicate the visual premise quickly; opening understandable without sound |

## Schwartz processes that carry this stage

- **Identification:** connect the product with a credible desired role, character, status or self-expression close to the existing self-image.
- **Gradualization:** begin with observations the audience accepts, then build a logical chain towards the new conclusion.
- **Camouflage:** adapt to the native format and language of the medium — while remaining clearly disclosed as advertising.
- **Mood:** rhythm, vocabulary and tone create emotion; match energy to audience and sophistication.

## Common mistakes

- Leading with the brand, discount and features before relevance exists.
- Inventing or exaggerating a problem the audience does not experience.
- Becoming so mysterious that the product connection disappears.
- Using generic curiosity that attracts people who can never become logical prospects.
- Judging the concept only by immediate last-click return.
