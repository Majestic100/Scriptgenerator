# Stage 4 · Product Aware

*The prospect's silent question: "Why this product, and is it right for me?"*

**Definition:** The person knows the brand or product and understands its general promise, but is not convinced about fit, credibility, value or superiority to alternatives.

**Primary-source nuance (Schwartz):** Seven tasks: reinforce desire; sharpen the image of satisfaction; extend where and when it satisfies; introduce new proof; announce a better mechanism; remove a former limitation; or change the product image or mechanism to escape direct competition. The product name may be prominent because awareness already exists.

## What is happening in the prospect's mind

This is the objection-and-proof condition. The person may have seen several ads, visited the product or spoken to the company. General education rarely creates progress; the remaining doubt must be identified and answered.

- Typical barriers: "Will it work for someone like me?", "Is the quality real?", "What is the catch?", "Is the price fair?", "Can I trust you?"
- The person evaluates risk, effort, delivery, experience and after-purchase support as well as features.
- Social proof is strongest when it matches the segment, use case and objection.
- High-ticket and B2B offers may require different proof for different stakeholders.

## The ad's single primary job

**Belief shift:** From "I know the product, but I am not sure" → To "It is credible, fits my situation and is preferable to the relevant alternatives."

Identify the specific objection and provide the proof that reduces it. Differentiation, demonstration, reviews, cases, guarantee, transparency and fit are more valuable than another broad problem hook.

## Ad architecture

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hook | Direct objection, review outcome, specific differentiator, case result or product demonstration |
| Claim | A precise benefit for a concrete segment or use case |
| Reason why | Mechanism, design choice, process or service standard that explains the benefit |
| Proof | Verifiable demonstration, customer case, data, review, third party or guarantee |
| Close | View pricing, read the case, try with risk reversal or book a consultation |

Default sequence: Objection → precise claim → reason why → matched proof → risk reduction. Typical CTA: view price, read case, book consultation.

## Hook and angle families (English patterns)

- "Can you hear the motor at night? Here it is without music or voice-over."
- "See the difference between a random reference mix and a design built for this placement."
- "Why we do not count a lead as a success until it is qualified."
- "What actually comes in the box? Here is every item."

## Danish phrasing patterns (write like this, do not translate)

- "Kan motoren høres om natten? Her er den uden musik og voice-over."
- "Se forskellen på et tilfældigt reference-mix og et design bygget til netop denne placering."
- "Hvorfor vi ikke måler et lead som en succes, før det er kvalificeret."
- "Det følger faktisk med - her er alt, der ligger i kassen."

## Creative, proof, CTA and destination

| Component | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Creative | UGC review, unboxing, raw demonstration, side-by-side, FAQ, objection handling, detailed case or founder explanation |
| Proof | Specific and relevant: verified reviews, documented process, customer before/after context, test, guarantee, clear terms |
| CTA | View the product, read reviews, see the case, request a price estimate or book a consultation |
| Destination | Product or service page with proof near claims and visible pricing, delivery, return, FAQ and next step |
| Trust | Independent review signals and realistic customer imagery beat generic selected testimonials |

## Schwartz processes that carry this stage

- **Verification:** proof placed adjacent to the doubt it resolves; specific and relevant beats broad and generic.
- **Concentration:** fair trade-off analysis against the standard alternative.
- **Intensification:** sharpen the image of satisfaction; first-day ownership story; product in action.
- **Redefinition:** change the price comparison to total value, useful life, alternative cost — show total price and assumptions, never hide continuing costs.

## Common mistakes

- Repeating problem education instead of resolving the final doubt.
- Using social proof without source, context or use-case match.
- Hiding price, terms or process until the person feels manipulated.
- Claiming "best" without a defined comparison or evidence.
