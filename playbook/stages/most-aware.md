# Stage 5 · Most Aware

*The prospect's silent question: "Why should I act now, and will it be easy and safe?"*

**Definition:** The person knows the product, understands what it does and wants or prefers it. The remaining barrier is usually price, timing, risk, availability, practical friction or procrastination.

**Primary-source nuance (Schwartz):** When the prospect knows the product, knows what it does and wants it, the ad may state little more than product name and bargain price. The remaining copy can summarise the most desirable selling points and add the store, coupon or direct response. News may be price, gift, immediate delivery or proximity.

## What is happening in the prospect's mind

Most Aware is NOT synonymous with every retargeted visitor. It is the condition in which further education may slow the purchase. The person needs clarity, reassurance and a concrete next step.

- Questions are operational: price, delivery, booking, returns, stock, payment and what happens next.
- A discount is optional; friction removal, availability or a meaningful bonus may be enough.
- Urgency must be real. Permanent countdowns and false scarcity damage trust.
- Checkout and form friction are part of the marketing argument.

## The ad's single primary job

**Belief shift:** From "I want it" → To "It makes sense to act now, and the next step feels safe and easy."

Make the offer, terms and action unmistakably clear. Remove the known final barrier and explain exactly what follows the click, purchase or booking.

## Ad architecture

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hook | Product name, price, offer, real deadline, stock, bonus, guarantee or availability |
| Value | Summarise the one to three decisive benefits — do not retell the entire sales argument |
| Risk reversal | Return, guarantee, deposit, rescheduling, delivery or process |
| Urgency | A real date, capacity, price change or stock constraint |
| Close | One clear action with precise expectation setting |

Default sequence: Product/offer → decisive value → terms → real urgency or availability. Typical CTA: buy, choose time, pay deposit.

## Hook and angle families (English patterns)

- "Save 10% on Numora until Sunday at 23:59."
- "Next available full-day sessions: 3, 8 and 12 September."
- "Your cart is still saved, and delivery is included."
- "Book the consultation now. Your deposit is deducted from the session price."

## Danish phrasing patterns (write like this, do not translate)

- "Spar 10 % på Numora frem til søndag kl. 23.59."
- "Næste ledige heldagssessioner: 3., 8. og 12. september."
- "Din kurv er stadig gemt - levering er inkluderet."
- "Book konsultationen nu. Depositummet fratrækkes din session."

## Creative, proof, CTA and destination

| Component | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Creative | Offer static, dynamic product ad, cart reminder, stock or availability, concise testimonial, FAQ close or deadline reminder |
| Proof | Brief reassurance: rating, guarantee, delivery, secure payment and concrete availability |
| CTA | Buy now, complete purchase, choose a time, pay deposit or book now |
| Destination | Directly to the relevant product variant, cart, checkout or calendar |
| Lead form | Use higher-intent review steps when quality matters more than maximum form volume |

## Schwartz processes that carry this stage

- **Redefinition:** remove the last price/timing/friction barrier without re-educating.
- **Verification:** brief, concrete reassurance at the point of action.
- **Concentrated intensification:** one to three decisive benefits, nothing more.

## Common mistakes

- Calling broad cold targeting Most Aware merely because the ad contains an offer.
- Creating false urgency or hiding the total price and terms.
- Sending ready-to-buy traffic to a generic long-form storytelling page.
- Optimising lead campaigns to cheap forms without feedback on qualification and sales.
