# Stage 2 · Problem Aware

*The prospect's silent question: "Why is this happening, and can it be solved?"*

**Definition:** The person recognises a problem, symptom, need or unfulfilled desire, but does not yet understand it fully or know the relevant solution category.

**Primary-source nuance (Schwartz):** Name the need and/or solution, dramatise the need until its importance is understood, then present the product as the natural solution.

## What is happening in the prospect's mind

The person is in diagnosis mode. They want accurate language for what is happening, a plausible explanation and evidence that improvement is possible. An immediate product pitch feels premature.

- Common emotions: frustration, uncertainty, fatigue, lost control and hope for improvement.
- The person may hold an inaccurate explanation; reframe it without insulting or shaming them.
- Consequences can create priority, but agitation must remain truthful and proportionate.
- The best message gives a useful mini-diagnosis and opens the door to a class of solutions.

## The ad's single primary job

**Belief shift:** From "I have this problem" → To "I understand it better, and a credible path forward exists."

Describe the problem more precisely than the prospect can, explain the relevant cause and show that a solution need not require what they fear. Introduce the solution only after that bridge exists.

## Ad architecture

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Hook | The symptom or frustration in customer language |
| Diagnosis | A simple explanation of the overlooked cause, error or trade-off |
| Cost | The real consequence in time, money, discomfort, uncertainty or missed outcome |
| Hope | The principle behind a solution without overwhelming with product details |
| Close | Invite the next learning step or a low-pressure assessment |

Default sequence: Symptom → diagnosis → consequence → plausible solution principle. Typical CTA: get guide, see options, receive assessment.

## Hook and angle families (English patterns)

- "If your baby settles with movement but wakes when it stops, the repetition may be the part exhausting you."
- "You do not need a finished tattoo design. That is exactly why the consultation exists."
- "More leads do not solve the problem if half are never contacted."
- "The colour is not always wrong. The light in the room may be changing it."

## Danish phrasing patterns (write like this, do not translate)

- "Hvis barnet falder til ro i bevægelse, men bliver uroligt, når du stopper, er det gentagelsen, der slider."
- "Du behøver ikke have det færdige tattoo-design. Det er præcis derfor, konsultationen findes."
- "Flere leads løser ikke problemet, hvis halvdelen aldrig bliver kontaktet."
- "Det er ikke altid farven, der er forkert. Lyset i rummet ændrer den."

## Creative, proof, CTA and destination

| Component | What usually works |
|---|---|
| Creative | Symptom list, myth correction, expert explanation, cause-led before/after, problem story or "three signs" |
| Proof | Expertise, visual demonstration, data or a short customer example that validates the diagnosis |
| CTA | Get the guide, see the solution, receive an assessment or explore the options |
| Destination | Problem-led page: recognition → diagnosis → solution principle → proof → low-pressure CTA |
| Form | Ask only for necessary information; explain the value before requesting personal data |

## Schwartz processes that carry this stage

- **Intensification:** present the dominant outcome through desire-building perspectives — demonstration, first-day story, before/after, contrast with the alternative's real friction.
- **Gradualization:** accepted reality → interpretation → mechanism → claim → proof; claim strength depends on the preparation before it.
- **Redefinition:** reduce perceived difficulty ("change the perceived task, make the first step smaller") — honestly.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Problem Aware as permission for an artificially negative fear ad.
- Describing a generic problem every competitor already names.
- Jumping from a symptom directly to "book a sales call".
- Promising a universal solution without explaining fit and limitations.
