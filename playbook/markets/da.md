# Output market: Danish (da)

**Rule:** Classification and strategy happen in English. The script itself is WRITTEN in Danish from scratch — natural, spoken, contemporary Danish as the target audience actually talks. Never translate English copy literally.

## What natural Danish ad language sounds like

- Short main clauses. Spoken rhythm. Contractions and everyday words ("slider", "op igen", "det følger faktisk med").
- Concrete over abstract: "alle de små gange, du skal op igen" — not "gentagne natlige afbrydelser".
- Direct address with "du/I", present tense, active voice.
- Numbers and terms in Danish convention: "10 %", "kl. 23.59", "3.900 kr.", dates as "3., 8. og 12. september".
- Real Danish idioms only — no anglicisms translated word for word ("det giver mening" is fine; "det er ikke raketvidenskab" borders on cliché; never "på slutningen af dagen").

## Translation artefacts to avoid

- English possessive constructions ("din rejse mod bedre søvn" — write "så du kan sove igennem igen").
- Title Case in Overlays ("Spar 20% I Dag" → "Spar 20 % i dag").
- "Unlock", "elevate", "game-changer" style verbs — Danish equivalents sound like marketing, use plain verbs.
- Exclamation inflation. Danish trust drops with every extra "!".

## Voice-of-customer discipline

If an audience analysis is supplied, reuse its exact wording, trade terms and quotes (e.g. "KS", "tilsyn", "fejl og mangler", "ryggen fri"). The audience's own words beat invented copy. Do not invent quotes or statistics that are not in the material.
