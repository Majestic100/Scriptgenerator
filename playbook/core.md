# Awareness Playbook — Core (always loaded)

Source: "Awareness Stages in Marketing — Strategic Reference Guide" (Hero Media, based on Eugene M. Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*). This file is the classification apparatus. The generator MUST classify the strategy before writing any copy.

**Core system instruction:** Use English for strategic classification and canonical terminology. Write the final advertising script in the requested market language, using natural voice-of-customer phrasing rather than translating English copy literally.

## Core idea

Awareness describes what the prospect already knows about the problem or desire, available solutions and your specific product. The right ad meets that existing knowledge and moves only the next necessary belief. A strong ad normally moves ONE blocking belief, not the prospect through all five stages in one script.

## The model at a glance

| Stage | What the prospect knows | Primary advertising task | Natural next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | At most, notices a situation, feeling, identity or latent desire | Make a relevant pattern visible | Watch, read or recognise |
| Problem Aware | Can name the pain, symptom or unmet need, but not the solution category | Clarify the problem and make a path plausible | Learn why or how |
| Solution Aware | Knows solution types exist and evaluates approaches | Establish criteria, mechanism and category fit | Investigate or compare |
| Product Aware | Knows the product or brand but is not convinced | Prove fit and remove the relevant objection | View proof, compare or request terms |
| Most Aware | Knows, understands and wants the product; a final barrier remains | Make action concrete, safe and timely | Buy, book or pay |

## What awareness is — and is not

Awareness is a knowledge-and-acceptance state. It is NOT:

| Concept | What it describes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness state | What the prospect knows and is ready to accept | Knows the problem but not the solution category |
| Funnel stage | How the business organises the relationship | Cold, warm, retargeting, lead, opportunity |
| Intent | What the person is attempting right now | Searching "best bedside crib" or visiting pricing |
| Platform objective | The outcome used to optimise delivery | Awareness, leads or sales |
| Market sophistication | How much similar marketing the market has already encountered | An exhausted market needs a credible mechanism, proof or new identification |

**Operational consequence:** A previous website visitor may still be Problem Aware. A completely new prospect may be Most Aware after a trusted recommendation. Temperature and awareness often correlate, but they are not the same variable. Awareness is not necessarily linear — prospects move forward, backward and sideways.

## Schwartz's strategic engine (order matters)

1. **Mass desire** — Which existing force does the market already want fulfilled? Copy channels desire; it does not manufacture it. Choose ONE dominant desire per ad; secondary benefits document or intensify the main appeal.
2. **State of awareness** — What can the prospect already recognise and accept? Choose the point of entry, explanatory burden and permitted directness.
3. **Market sophistication** — How many similar claims and mechanisms has the market already seen? Decide whether a simple promise is fresh, or mechanism/proof/positioning/identification must carry the difference.

## The five levels of market sophistication

| Level | Market condition | Message strategy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · First | Few comparable claims are known | State the need and promise simply and directly |
| 2 · Enlarged claim | Competitors repeat the promise and escalate it | Make an accepted claim more specific or stronger — only within what can be documented |
| 3 · New mechanism | Large claims have lost credibility | Shift emphasis from what the outcome is to HOW a new mechanism can produce it |
| 4 · Elaborated mechanism | Competitors have copied the mechanism language | Make the mechanism more concrete, easier, faster or free from a known limitation |
| 5 · Identification | The market is tired of claims and mechanisms | Enter through experience, identity, worldview and situation; let the product follow |

**Modern constraint:** Sophistication is not permission to make larger unsupported claims. In a mature market, the responsible response is greater precision, transparency, demonstration and relevant proof — not greater hype.

## Diagnosis: decision sequence

1. Can the person describe the problem, need or desired outcome? If no: **Unaware**.
2. Can the person name a solution category or approach? If no: **Problem Aware**.
3. Does the person know the named brand or product as a concrete option? If no: **Solution Aware**.
4. Is the person convinced that this product is the right fit and worth the trade-offs? If no: **Product Aware**.
5. Has the choice effectively been made, while price, timing, risk, availability or friction still blocks action? If yes: **Most Aware**.

**Diagnostic rule:** The stage is determined by the missing belief — not by the ad format the team would prefer to produce, and not by targeting alone.

## Classification guardrails

- Unaware ≠ cold traffic. It is an absence of recognised or admitted problem/desire knowledge.
- Problem Aware requires recognised need or pain but no established solution category.
- Solution Aware requires awareness of possible methods or categories, even when the named product is unknown.
- Product Aware requires knowledge of the named product or brand as an option; the missing belief concerns fit, superiority, value, trust or risk.
- Most Aware requires both product knowledge and substantial desire or preference. A site visitor, video viewer or retargeting audience does not automatically qualify.
- When evidence supports more than one state, produce separate script hypotheses instead of forcing one hybrid script.

## Permitted assumptions by stage

| Stage | The script MAY assume | The script MUST NOT assume |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | A familiar situation, identity, emotion or latent desire supported by research | Problem recognition, category knowledge, product knowledge or price readiness |
| Problem Aware | Recognition of the pain, need or desired change | Understanding of solution categories or preference for the product |
| Solution Aware | Knowledge that methods or categories exist | Knowledge of the named product or belief in its superiority |
| Product Aware | Knowledge of the named product and its basic promise | Conviction about fit, proof, value, trust or superiority |
| Most Aware | Knowledge, desire and practical purchase readiness | That urgency, scarcity or price advantage exists unless supplied as fact |

## Mandatory reasoning sequence (before writing)

1. **Extract facts** — Separate supplied facts from interpretations, assumptions and missing information.
2. **Diagnose awareness** — Classify the missing belief using the decision sequence; do not classify from targeting alone.
3. **Assess sophistication** — Estimate how familiar and exhausted the category's claims and mechanisms are. Mark uncertainty when market evidence is unavailable.
4. **Select mass desire** — Choose one dominant existing desire that the product can legitimately channel.
5. **Define belief shift** — One sentence describing current belief, one describing the belief required after the ad.
6. **Choose angle and process** — Select the message angle and relevant Schwartz process based on the missing belief.
7. **Match mechanism and proof** — Use only mechanisms and proof supported by the supplied product facts.
8. **Set CTA intensity** — Choose the smallest natural next action that advances the decision.
9. **Write in market language** — Natural customer language in the requested output language; never translate English copy literally.
10. **Run QA** — Check stage assumptions, claim support, proof match, clarity, relevance, disclosure and message continuity.

## Required strategy output before the script

Awareness state | Evidence for classification | Market sophistication | Confidence level | Dominant mass desire | Current belief | Required belief shift | Primary angle | Schwartz process | Mechanism | Proof type | CTA | Unsupported claims excluded

If critical information is missing, state the assumption or request the information rather than fabricate customer insight, proof or urgency.

## Generator QA checklist

- The hook does not assume more knowledge than the selected state permits.
- The script moves one primary belief and does not attempt to compress every stage.
- The dominant desire is visible, and secondary benefits reinforce it.
- The mechanism explains the claim rather than merely renaming it.
- The proof answers the specific claim and objection; no fabricated statistics, reviews, experts, scarcity or case results.
- Market sophistication changes the expression without changing the awareness classification.
- The CTA commitment is natural for the state and business model.
- The final language sounds native to the output market and avoids literal translation artefacts.
- Claims, prices, deadlines, comparisons and limitations are supportable from supplied facts.
